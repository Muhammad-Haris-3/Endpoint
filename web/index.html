A census of 126,760 completed trials

Trials write down what they will measure. Then most of them never report it.

Every interventional trial declares its primary outcome before it enrols anyone, and ClinicalTrials.gov keeps every edit to that declaration, dated, in public. Endpoint reads the whole registry: what was promised, what changed, and what was never reported at all.

What was reported

Results reporting is carried in the frozen frame itself, so this section is a census, not a sample. Every trial counted here has a primary completion date between 2015 and 2022 and reached an end state, so it is more than three years past the 12-month deadline — an absent result is settled, not pending.

What this is not

This is not a count of legal violations. The FDA Final Rule's 12-month deadline applies to applicable clinical trials, and whether a given trial is one is not exposed by the API and is not adjudicated here. The claim is “no results posted”, never “in breach”.

Why the registry's own flag is not the answer

ClinicalTrials.gov records which version last modified the primary outcome. Taking that at face value gives a large, clean number — and on a 400-trial pilot, 37.7% of it did not survive reading the actual text. For one flagged trial in seven the outcome set was byte-identical across the change the registry flagged; others were capitalisation passes applied when results were attached.

So the flag is reported here as the flag, and the adjudicated figure is reported only once every flagged pair has been fetched and diffed. They are different numbers and the site does not blur them.

The participant count, and why it is not the headline

The pre-registered figure is the sum of enrolment across every trial that reported nothing. It is a count rather than an estimate, which is exactly why it was meant to lead this page. It cannot.

One record is a fifth of the figure

The single largest contributor is NCT05438901, “Investigation of Oxidant-antioxidant Status in Patients Treated With Hirudotherapy” — a single-group before/after study of leech therapy — recorded as an ACTUAL enrolment of 12,317,546. That is not a plausible count for that design, and it alone is over 21% of the pre-registered total.

Most remaining top contributors are behavioural megastudies — SMS nudges, online health advertisements, vaccine-booking reminders. They genuinely enrolled millions. But “enrolled in a clinical trial that never reported its results” invites you to picture someone taking an experimental drug, not someone receiving a text message.

Depending on the estimator, this figure ranges from 4.8 million to 58.7 million. The pre-registered one is shown unmodified and no substitution has been made, because choosing an estimator after seeing which reads better is the move a pre-registration exists to prevent.

What is not claimed

A retrospective change is not fraud. Registrations are edited for many legitimate reasons, including at a regulator's instruction. This site reports what changed and when, never why, and never intent.

Non-reporting is not a legal violation. FDAAA applicability is not exposed by the API and is not adjudicated here.

The adjudicator is a floor, not a ceiling. It compares text lexically. It cannot tell that “HbA1c at 12 weeks” and “glycated haemoglobin at 3 months” are one endpoint, nor that a promoted secondary outcome is a switch.
